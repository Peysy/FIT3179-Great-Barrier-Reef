3. Beneath the Surface: Environmental Change

Marine Park zoning helps manage human activities, but zoning is only part of the reef's environmental story. Coral cover can also change because of marine heat stress, storms and crown-of-thorns starfish.

Data integrity: A historical coral-cover chart is not included here because no verified year-by-year coral-cover dataset was supplied. Rather than invent a trend, this section identifies the monitoring evidence needed for a sound comparison.

For the environmental analysis, use the Australian Institute of Marine Science (AIMS) Long-Term Monitoring Program. A suitable next chart would compare measured hard coral cover for the Northern, Central and Southern Great Barrier Reef across consistent reporting years.

Monitoring source: AIMS Great Barrier Reef monitoring.

4. What Does This Mean for Reef Tourism?

The map shows how marine activities are managed across the Great Barrier Reef. The tourism charts show the importance of domestic travel and a diverse set of international visitor markets in nearby Tropical North Queensland.

These datasets alone cannot show whether tourism has caused a change in coral health. A defensible sustainability analysis would require consistent reef-level visitation measures alongside independently verified environmental monitoring data.
