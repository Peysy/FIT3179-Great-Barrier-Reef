The Hidden Environmental Cost of Reef Tourism

While tourism contributes to regional economies, recreational diving can also place physical pressure on coral reefs.

A 2026 study involving 732 divers in Indonesia and the Philippines found that many damaging interactions were unintentional or went unnoticed.

Source: Lin et al. (2026). Observations were collected in Indonesia and the Philippines, not the Great Barrier Reef.

When Human Activities Threaten the Reef

While the Great Barrier Reef supports a thriving tourism industry, activities involving vessels can also pose environmental risks. Vessel groundings occur when boats come into contact with the seabed or coral reefs, potentially damaging marine habitats.

Recreational vessels consistently recorded more groundings than commercial fishing and tourism vessels between 2019–20 and 2022–23. However, these figures represent reported incidents rather than the relative risk of each vessel type.

Source: Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority (2024), Outlook Report, Figure 5.16. Values approximately digitised from the published figure.

How Is Coral Cover Changing?

Coral cover is an important indicator of reef condition. Monitoring across the Great Barrier Reef reveals differences in coral cover between its northern, central and southern regions.

Between 2025 and 2026, average hard coral cover increased in the Northern and Central GBR, while the Southern GBR experienced a slight decline. These changes reflect multiple environmental influences and cannot be attributed solely to tourism.

Source: Australian Institute of Marine Science (AIMS), Annual Summary Report 2025–2026.

When Storms Strike the Reef

Tropical cyclones are a major source of natural disturbance across the Great Barrier Reef. Strong winds and waves can damage coral structures, while heavy rainfall can increase sediment runoff. Explore the paths of tropical cyclones that travelled near the Great Barrier Reef between 2020 and 2025.

3. Beneath the Surface: Environmental Change

Marine Park zoning helps manage human activities, but zoning is only part of the reef's environmental story. Coral cover can also change because of marine heat stress, storms and crown-of-thorns starfish.

Data integrity: A historical coral-cover chart is not included here because no verified year-by-year coral-cover dataset was supplied. Rather than invent a trend, this section identifies the monitoring evidence needed for a sound comparison.
